Questions

Top questions

01Why a Fractional CCO when we already have a Commercial Director or VP Sales?

Your Commercial Director or VP Sales runs the operation. A CCO designs the structure that operation runs inside. Different jobs. A commercial leader keeps the current motion productive. A CCO decides whether that motion is the one that still works at three times current revenue, and rebuilds it early enough that growth is not disrupted.

02Two to three days per week — is that enough for a CCO role?

For change work, yes. For daily management, no — that stays with your team. A fractional CCO buys senior judgment on the levers that move the business: hiring, coverage, forecast integrity, board reporting, the top ten accounts. If I am spending time on inbox triage, both of us are losing money.

03How does an engagement start?

With a free thirty-minute call. If there is a fit, you get a short written proposal within a week — scope, timing and fee. Most engagements start with a Commercial Sprint, and about half of Sprints don't extend. That is the right outcome when the plan can be built in-house.

Questions

Anticipated objections

01Why a Fractional CCO when we already have a Commercial Director or VP Sales?

Your Commercial Director or VP Sales runs the operation. A CCO designs the structure that operation runs inside. Different jobs. A commercial leader keeps the current motion productive. A CCO decides whether that motion is the one that still works at three times current revenue, and rebuilds it early enough that growth is not disrupted.

02Why Europe specifically?

Because Europe is where the mistake most non-European SaaS companies make is largest and most expensive. You are entering not one market but twenty-seven, each with its own buying culture, procurement pattern, language, and legal reality. A generalist CCO treats Europe as one region. It is not.

03Two to three days per week — is that enough for a CCO role?

For change work, yes. For daily management, no — that stays with your team. A fractional CCO buys senior judgment on the levers that move the business: hiring, coverage, forecast integrity, board reporting, the top ten accounts. If I am spending time on inbox triage, both of us are losing money.

04How does an engagement start?

With a free thirty-minute call. If there is a fit, you get a short written proposal within a week — scope, timing and fee. Most engagements start with a Commercial Sprint, and about half of Sprints don't extend. That is the right outcome when the plan can be built in-house.

05When is a Fractional CCO the wrong choice?

When you need a full-time seat that will not leave. When revenue is under one million euro and every day of executive time is still a founder day. When the actual problem is the product — no CCO fixes a product-market gap by hiring more sellers. And when the CEO is not ready to hand real decision rights to someone brought in part-time.

06What does it cost?

It depends on scope, format and stage. A Sprint has a fixed fee, fractional work a monthly retainer, interim a day rate. You get a clear figure in the proposal after our first conversation. For CCO work I don't take equity instead of fees.

07Can you also make introductions?

Yes. Within an engagement my network is part of what you get — first enterprise doors, senior talent, partners, investors. Outside an engagement I take on a few introductions a year, on request, success-based and agreed up front.

08What happens at the end of a CCO engagement?

A permanent European leader is in seat, onboarded, with a system that works around them. Written playbooks, forecast cadence, hire scorecards, region-specific ICP and messaging, board reporting rhythm — all documented and handed over. If Europe collapses back to where it was, the transfer was not done. That is my failure, not the successor's.

Approach

The European Playbook

How I build a European commercial function: the pattern I keep seeing, the four pillars I work on, how an engagement runs — and the network that comes with it.

Summarize this page with:
Position
Not a consultant leaving a report. Not an interim keeping the seat warm. An operator — brought in to build the European commercial function and hand it back working.
01 · Pattern

You will recognise one of these

01
Home compounds. Europe doesn't.
US or UK revenue keeps growing. European deals take twice as long to close at half the win rate. DACH pipeline is thin, Nordics won't return calls. Your Head of Sales calls it a market problem. It rarely is.
02
The European hire didn't work.
You hired a European lead six months ago. Pipeline is thin, forecast keeps slipping, reporting is a story rather than a system. You cannot tell if the person is wrong for the role or the role is wrong for the person.
03
Investor pressure without a playbook.
Series A or B closed on an international thesis. The board wants twelve months of European proof. Your CCO seat is empty, or filled by someone who has never opened a market outside the home country.
Three symptoms. One common cause — the European revenue function was never actually built. Only a hire was made and a target was set.
02 · Method

Four pillars, in order

Four pillars, in order. A weak pillar makes the whole region fragile.

Most scale-ups treat European expansion as a hiring problem — find the right country manager, growth follows. It doesn't. Europe is not one market but a system of markets, each with its own buying culture, procurement pattern, and legal shape.

Pillar 01
Market
Where do we actually win?
ICP tested against the buying reality of each European sub-region, not copied from home. Segmentation, competitor position, price sensitivity, where sales cycles are shortest.
Pillar 02
Motion
How do we sell it?
Founder-led, inbound, outbound, channel or partner — chosen deliberately for the region. Contract shape, procurement patterns, GDPR and data residency, VAT reality.
Pillar 03
Team
Who executes it?
Coverage model, hiring sequence, comp plan, quota architecture, coaching cadence. Where to base the hub, who to hire first, why the wrong first hire delays Europe by a year.
Pillar 04
Cadence
How do we know it works?
Forecast discipline, pipeline hygiene, operating rhythm, board reporting. European signal built early, not scrambled together before each board meeting.
03 · In practice

How an engagement runs

I
Diagnose
Weeks 1–4
Pipeline, team, systems and market fit. Interviews with founders, sales and customers. A ranked diagnosis on the four pillars.
II
Plan
Weeks 4–7
A written build plan: segments, motion per country, first hires, comp, cadence and 90-day milestones. Reviewed with founder and board.
III
Run
Months 2–9
I run the region: hiring the team, closing the first European accounts, installing the operating rhythm, reporting to the board.
IV
Hand over
Final 1–2 months
Transfer to a permanent leader, recruited alongside me and onboarded into a working system. Done when Europe runs without me.
